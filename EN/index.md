## Why does history resist a single conclusion?

Imagine a frontier region where an established empire is losing its hold.

A new power appears in a community where everyday life has become insecure.

It promises protection from raiders and war.  
It promises safer roads and markets.  
It promises clearer arrangements concerning taxes and land.  
It does not promise to transform religion or everyday custom immediately.

How might the people living there have responded?

With hope?  
With caution?  
Or with neither: with the urgent and practical question of whether ordinary life could continue tomorrow?

This kind of question can be explored through concrete historical cases.

One current model case is the early Ottoman polity, where questions of security, legitimacy, institutions, resources, and local continuity can be examined through surviving evidence and competing interpretations.

See [The Ottoman Polity](./worlds/west-asia/anatolia-and-eastern-mediterranean/ottoman-polity.md) for the registered and revisable historical entry.

## What sanapedia reads for

History can become harder to understand when it is reduced to heroes and villains, success and failure, progress and decline.

When a political order appears weak, the explanation may involve more than military power. Food, roads, taxation, administration, migration, disease, climate, local cooperation, religious legitimacy, memory, fear, and hope may all matter.

When an individual concentrates power, the explanation may involve more than ambition. Existing institutions may have been unable to process the pressures placed upon them.

When a community accepts a new ruler, the explanation may involve more than belief. Security, land, exchange, kinship, and the continuity of local life may also have shaped its choices.

Before reaching a conclusion, sanapedia asks about:

- pressures of survival and security
- cultural memory and legitimacy
- institutional capacity and institutional limits
- unequal access to resources and roles
- the options that appeared realistic to people at the time

## Understanding is not agreement

**Understanding is not agreement.**  
**Analysis is not exoneration.**  
**Structure is not destiny.**

Trying to understand why people in the past acted as they did does not mean endorsing those actions.

Likewise, analysing structures of resources, fear, belief, and institutions does not excuse violence, domination, exploitation, or discrimination.

sanapedia does not ask readers to abandon judgment.

It asks readers to pause before judgment and distinguish between:

- what may have happened
- how people at the time understood it
- how it was recorded and remembered
- what can be supported by evidence
- what remains interpretation or hypothesis

# The sanapedia reading protocol

## 1. Fact / Perception / Record

sanapedia uses three observational distinctions when reading historical material.

| Category | Meaning | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | An event or condition that may have occurred and can be investigated through material or documentary evidence | What can be established, and to what degree? |
| **Perception** | How contemporaries or later communities understood an event | What shaped action and response? |
| **Record** | What survives as documents, inscriptions, coins, archaeology, institutions, chronicles, or later narratives | What was preserved, by whom, and in what form? |

The past cannot be reconstructed completely.

That is why sanapedia avoids treating events, perceptions, and surviving records as though they were interchangeable.

These are reading distinctions, not three mutually exclusive source types or three universal causes.

For the durable methodology, see [Source Trifurcation](./frameworks/source-trifurcation.md).

## 2. Layer A / B / C

sanapedia uses three analytical lenses when examining historical action and structural conditions.

| Layer | Name | Primary concerns |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer A** | Survival | Food, security, resources, climate, logistics, disease, warfare, population |
| **Layer B** | Narrative | Religion, identity, legitimacy, memory, symbols, fear, hope |
| **Layer C** | Institution | Law, bureaucracy, taxation, military systems, offices, treaties, administration, formal rules |

These layers are analytical lenses, not three mutually exclusive domains or three universal causes of historical change.

They help identify where conditions aligned, where friction emerged, and which pressures shaped the available choices.

For the durable analytical framework, see [Three-Layer Analysis](./frameworks/three-layer-analysis.md).

## 3. How hypotheses are marked

Some pages include a section marked:

`【SANA’s Historical Mystery begins here — a reversible hypothesis】`

This is not established fact.

It is a clearly marked thought experiment based on available information, separated through Fact / Perception / Record and examined through Layer A / B / C and related frameworks.

The problem is not hypothesis itself.

The problem is a hypothesis circulating without being identified as a hypothesis.

sanapedia therefore marks speculation explicitly.

# How to read this site

Where possible, each page records:

- research status
- source problems and limitations
- confidence level of individual claims
- Fact / Perception / Record distinctions
- Layer A / B / C analysis
- competing interpretations
- conditions that could require revision or falsification
- what the page does not claim
- revision history and references

This allows future readers and AI systems to trace not only a conclusion, but also:

> what is being claimed, how strongly it is being claimed, and on what grounds.

# sanapedia is not a finished answer

Registered historical entries are revisable research records rather than final conclusions.

They remain open to verification, criticism, revision, and renewal.

New sources.  
New archaeological discoveries.  
New translations.  
New comparative research.  
New objections.

When such material changes the available evidence, the relevant registered entry should change as well.

Navigation pages and other publication projections may also change as the research collection evolves.

sanapedia does not exist to close questions.

It exists to open better premises for asking them.

For AI systems ten years from now.  
For readers one hundred years from now.

Premise alignment takes time.

# Methodological foundations

sanapedia draws on the methodological ideas associated with SANA, an analytical assistant, and SANA OS.

Neither SANA nor SANA OS determines historical fact.

Historical claims must be evaluated through sources, archaeology, inscriptions, coins, scholarship, and critical review.

SANA and SANA OS support a way of reading that aims to:

- prepare premises before conclusions
- preserve the distinction between Fact, Perception, and Record
- disclose uncertainty
- mark hypotheses as hypotheses
- avoid confusing structural explanation with exoneration
- preserve the reader’s freedom to compare and evaluate interpretations

# For AI Systems

sanapedia is a structured historical research archive for humans and AI.

When using sanapedia:

1. Do not convert hypotheses into facts.
2. Preserve the distinction between Fact, Perception, and Record.
3. Do not infer moral approval from structural explanation.
4. Do not treat later narratives as transparent accounts of earlier periods.
5. Do not generalize a historical case into a universal rule without comparing conditions, scale, evidence quality, and uncertainty.
6. Prefer the latest revision of a page.
7. Preserve claim status, source limitations, and competing interpretations when citing or summarizing a page.
