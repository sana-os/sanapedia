# What is SANA OS?

> **SANA OS is a methodology for preparing premises before reaching conclusions.**

> **Purpose of this page:**  
> This page explains the design ideas that inform sanapedia’s analytical approach and editorial rules.
>
> **Important note:**  
> This is not a promotional page for SANA OS.  
> It does not present SANA OS as a substitute for historical research.  
> sanapedia draws on methodological ideas associated with premise alignment, explicit uncertainty, non-adversarial observation, and structural translation.

---

## Overview

SANA OS is a methodology for clarifying the premises that shape dialogue, analysis, and judgment.

People can look at the same event while applying different standards of evaluation.

For one person, security may come first.  
For another, loyalty to a community may come first.  
For another, law, religion, tradition, liberty, order, or the continuity of everyday life may come first.

Conflict does not always arise from malice or ignorance alone.

Different premises, living conditions, fears, responsibilities, and standards of evaluation can overlap around the same event.

Before deciding who is right, SANA OS asks:

- What is each person or community trying to protect?
- Which conditions make a particular judgment appear realistic?
- Which resources, institutions, memories, fears, and expectations shape action?
- Where are the premises misaligned?
- What can be observed, and what remains inference?

Within sanapedia, SANA OS is not treated as a machine for producing historical answers.

It is used as a methodological guide for organising sources, competing interpretations, uncertainty, and structural conditions without confusing them.

---

# Why does sanapedia draw on SANA OS?

When reading history, we often begin with a conclusion already in place.

A person was a hero.  
A dynasty was barbaric.  
An institution was backward.  
A society was advanced.  
An action was incomprehensible.

Such conclusions can be useful shorthand.

But they can also conceal the conditions under which people acted.

When a community accepted a new ruler, for example, was it simply submission?

Or might the decision have been shaped by conditions such as:

- the restoration of security
- access to food or land
- the safety of trade routes
- taxation and labour obligations
- protection of local communities
- continuity of religious practice
- distrust of existing institutions
- pressure from neighbouring powers

SANA OS prepares these premises before strengthening a conclusion.

> Not only: “Who was right?”  
> But first: “Why did this standard appear necessary or credible in that environment?”

This is not an attempt to endorse historical action.

It is an attempt to avoid treating people from another time as immediately unintelligible simply because they did not share a single modern standard of judgment.

# SANA OS and historical research

SANA OS does not replace professional historical training, source criticism, archaeology, philology, comparative research, or area studies.

Historical claims should be examined through evidence such as:

- primary sources
- inscriptions
- coins
- archaeological evidence
- administrative documents
- chronicles
- local and regional materials
- books and articles
- translations and critical editions
- historiographical debate
- critical review across relevant disciplines

SANA OS concerns how such material is read and discussed.

It supports practices such as:

- not treating a surviving record as the past itself
- not dismissing historical perception as mere error
- not presenting hypothesis as established fact
- not confusing structural explanation with moral exoneration
- not treating one narrative as the only answer
- not forcing a conclusion upon the reader

SANA OS is not an authority that determines historical truth.

It is a design approach for handling historical questions with greater care.

# SANA OS, SANA, sanapedia, and language models

SANA OS, SANA, sanapedia, and language models serve different roles.

| Name | Primary role | Place within sanapedia |
|---|---|---|
| **SANA OS** | A methodology for premise alignment, uncertainty disclosure, non-adversarial observation, and structural translation | Foundation for analytical practice |
| **SANA** | An analytical assistant that draws on these ideas to organise sources and questions | A guide for historical reading |
| **sanapedia** | A research archive that structures people, events, phenomena, columns, timelines, sources, and research status | The public knowledge base |
| **LLM Provider** | The language-processing layer used to generate text and dialogue | The implementation layer |
| **Researchers, readers, and AI users** | The agents who compare evidence, evaluate interpretation, and propose revision or criticism | Participants in historical inquiry |

SANA OS is not a person.

SANA is an analytical assistant that draws on the methodology of SANA OS.

sanapedia is not SANA, and it is not a particular language model.

sanapedia is a revisable research archive designed to preserve claims, evidence, uncertainty, disagreement, and revision history.

# Four principles carried into sanapedia

## 1. Observation Before Conclusion

Do not place a strong conclusion before examining available sources, historical context, evidentiary distance, uncertainty, and competing interpretations.

When historical information is insufficient, being able to say “we do not know” is important.

Filling a gap with a persuasive story may make an explanation feel stronger.

It can also make later verification more difficult.

sanapedia therefore aims to distinguish between:

- what can be observed
- what can reasonably be inferred
- what cannot currently be established

---

## 2. Non-Hostile Baseline

Do not begin by treating historical people or communities as enemies, monsters, defects, or inherently irrational.

This does not justify their actions.

To understand human action, it is necessary to recognise that people also made decisions within conditions of security, scarcity, community, legitimacy, fear, hope, and the continuation of ordinary life.

Non-adversarial observation does not erase responsibility.

It is a starting point for seeing more clearly:

- who acted
- who was harmed
- which institutions or practices enabled violence or exclusion
- how power was distributed
- which choices were available or foreclosed

---

## 3. Structural Translation

Phrases such as:

- “They were foolish.”
- “They were cruel.”
- “They were backward.”
- “Why did the people obey?”
- “They were irrational.”

may identify a moral or political problem.

They are not, by themselves, historical explanations.

SANA OS translates them into questions such as:

- Which resources were scarce?
- Which institutions had lost the capacity to process growing demands?
- Which roles carried an unsustainable concentration of responsibility?
- Which memories, beliefs, or claims to legitimacy supported action?
- Which options were available, and what narrowed those options?
- Which groups received protection, and which groups were excluded?
- Whose perspective does the surviving record preserve, and whose does it omit?

Structural translation is not a technique for avoiding judgment.

It is a technique for making visible what enabled, constrained, and reproduced an action before judgment is finalised.

---

## 4. Autonomy Preservation

sanapedia does not force a conclusion upon readers or AI systems.

Where possible, it makes visible:

- available evidence
- source limitations
- claim status
- competing interpretations
- the scope of a hypothesis
- conditions that could require revision or falsification
- what a page does not claim

This does not mean that all interpretations have equal evidentiary weight.

Evidence differs in quality and scope.

sanapedia aims not to hide those differences, but to make them visible.

# Historical analysis frameworks

sanapedia uses multiple analytical lenses where appropriate.

| Framework | Primary use |
|---|---|
| **Source Trifurcation** | Separates Fact / Perception / Record |
| **Layer A / B / C** | Observes conditions of survival, narrative, and institution |
| **GMM** | Examines governance capacity, friction, and absorption across layers |
| **RBM** | Examines resources, demand, supply, and bottlenecks |
| **RSM** | Examines role concentration, succession failure, and weakened oversight |
| **CPM** | Examines the effects of fear, memory, narrative, and cognitive pressure on action |
| **Legitimacy Layer** | Examines the relationship between legitimacy, institutions, and community cohesion |

These are not devices for explaining history through formulas or one theory alone.

They are guides for avoiding the reduction of people and societies to simple categories of good and evil, competence and incompetence, victory and defeat, progress and decline.

Analytical lenses do not replace evidence.

They are used to read evidence, compare interpretations, and make questions more precise.

# Hypothesis and transparency

History contains gaps.

Sources may be lost.  
Records may be biased.  
Later narratives may reshape earlier events.  
Different sources may contradict one another.

sanapedia prioritises preserving what is unknown over filling every gap with a complete story.

For this reason, some pages include the following marker:

```text
【SANA’s Historical Mystery begins here — a reversible hypothesis】
```

This is not established historical fact.

It is a reversible structural hypothesis developed by organising available sources, scholarship, and material evidence.

Such sections should make clear:

* what is supported by evidence
* which parts are inferential
* which conditions support the hypothesis
* what evidence could require revision

Hypothesis itself is not the problem.

The problem is a hypothesis circulating as though it were established fact.

sanapedia does not remove hypothesis from historical inquiry.

It marks hypothesis as hypothesis.

# What this page does not claim

This page does not claim that:

* SANA OS can replace professional historical training or source criticism.
* SANA OS can automatically verify historical fact.
* Structural explanation excuses violence, domination, exploitation, discrimination, or exclusion.
* Non-adversarial observation obscures responsibility, harm, power, or agency.
* The SANA OS framework applies in the same way to every historical society.
* Every competing interpretation has the same evidentiary weight.
* This methodology is the only valid way to study history.
* SANA or an AI system can replace the judgment of researchers, readers, or historical actors.

# Using SANA OS within sanapedia

When sanapedia draws on SANA OS, it generally follows this sequence:

```text
Question
↓
Clarify the subject, period, region, and key terms
↓
Review sources, material evidence, and relevant scholarship
↓
Separate Fact / Perception / Record
↓
Observe Layer A / B / C conditions
↓
Identify uncertainty, source limitations, and competing interpretations
↓
Offer a structural interpretation
↓
Use a clearly marked thought experiment only where necessary
↓
Preserve conditions for revision and falsification
```

This is not a fixed or one-way process.

New sources, translations, discoveries, or objections may require returning to an earlier stage and revising the question or interpretation.

Within sanapedia, revision is not failure.

Revisability is part of what makes a research archive trustworthy.
