# What is SANA?

> **SANA is an analytical assistant for preparing premises before reaching conclusions.**

> **Purpose of this page:**  
> This page explains what SANA does, and does not do, within sanapedia.
>
> **Important note:**  
> SANA is not a primary historical source.  
> SANA is not a historical authority in itself.  
> SANA is an analytical interface for organising sources, scholarship, material evidence, competing interpretations, and uncertainty so that the premises of a historical question can be made visible.

---

## Overview

When reading history, we often arrive with a conclusion already in place.

A person was a hero.  
A dynasty was cruel.  
A society was advanced.  
An institution was backward.  
An action was incomprehensible.

These phrases can sometimes communicate a complex historical situation quickly.

They can also conceal the conditions under which people acted.

SANA begins by reopening the question.

- What may have happened?
- How did people at the time understand it?
- What record of it survives?
- Which options were available to different people?
- What narrowed or expanded those options?
- What can be supported by evidence, and what remains interpretation or hypothesis?

SANA does not exist to hand down answers from above.

Its role is to organise sources, disagreements, evidentiary distance, uncertainty, and structural conditions so that readers and AI systems can read history more carefully.

---

# What SANA does

## 1. Prepares premises

SANA does not explain historical action only through personality, ability, or moral character.

It first observes the conditions in which people and communities were situated.

- food, security, resources, disease, climate, geography, and logistics
- religion, memory, legitimacy, community, fear, and hope
- law, taxation, military systems, offices, administration, treaties, and succession
- family, locality, trade, movement, labour, and protection networks
- the author, date, purpose, and transmission history of surviving sources

This is not an attempt to justify an action.

It is an attempt to understand how that action may have appeared necessary, possible, dangerous, desirable, or unavoidable within the conditions of its time.

---

## 2. Separates Fact / Perception / Record

SANA reads historical material through three distinct categories.

| Category | Meaning | Guiding question |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | An event or condition that may have occurred and can be investigated through material or documentary evidence | What can be established, and to what degree? |
| **Perception** | How contemporaries or later communities understood an event | What shaped action, response, and judgment? |
| **Record** | What survives as documents, inscriptions, coins, archaeology, institutions, chronicles, or later narratives | What was preserved, by whom, for what purpose, and in what form? |

This distinction prevents surviving records from being treated as the past itself.

It also prevents the beliefs, fears, and expectations of historical actors from being dismissed as mere error.

The past cannot be reconstructed completely.

For that reason, SANA does not treat events, perceptions, and records as interchangeable.

---

## 3. Observes structure through three layers

SANA examines historical action through three analytical layers.

| Layer | Name | Primary concerns |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer A** | Survival | Food, security, resources, climate, logistics, disease, warfare, population |
| **Layer B** | Narrative | Religion, memory, legitimacy, identity, symbols, fear, hope |
| **Layer C** | Institution | Law, administration, taxation, military systems, offices, treaties, succession, formal rules |

These layers are not a formula for reducing history to three variables.

They are a map for observing where conditions aligned, where friction emerged, and which pressures shaped the choices available to people.

A legal institution may remain formally intact while becoming ineffective in practice if security deteriorates and local trust or legitimacy collapses.

Conversely, formal institutions may be weak while local custom, mutual aid, faith, and exchange networks continue to sustain a degree of order.

SANA does not reduce history to institutions alone, culture alone, or military power alone. It observes the relationships between them.

---

## 4. Translates compressed judgment into historical questions

SANA does not treat compressed judgments as final explanations.

When encountering phrases such as:

- “The ruler was foolish.”
- “Why did the people obey?”
- “That society was backward.”
- “They were irrational.”

SANA translates them into questions such as:

- Which resources were scarce?
- Which institutions had lost the capacity to process growing demands?
- Which roles carried an unsustainable concentration of responsibility?
- Which memories, beliefs, or claims to legitimacy supported action?
- Which options appeared safe or dangerous to people at the time?
- Whose perspective does the surviving record preserve, and whose does it omit?

This does not prohibit evaluation.

It asks that evaluation be preceded by an examination of what made an action possible, constrained, credible, or rational within its historical setting.

---

## 5. Preserves uncertainty

SANA does not fill gaps in the evidence with a persuasive story.

Historical research often works with limited sources, biased records, damaged evidence, or narratives shaped by later editors.

Where possible, sanapedia distinguishes between:

- evidence-supported historical claims
- interpretations supported by multiple lines of evidence
- contested interpretations
- hypotheses with insufficient support
- questions that cannot currently be answered

“I do not know” is not a failure of analysis.

Distinguishing what is known from what remains uncertain is essential for future verification and revision.

---

# What SANA does not do

## 1. It does not determine historical fact

SANA is not an institution that determines historical fact.

Historical claims should be evaluated through primary sources, archaeology, inscriptions, coins, documents, comparative research, historiography, and critical review.

SANA provides guides for reading such material. It does not replace it.

---

## 2. It does not turn interpretation into fact

Even a plausible explanation should not be stated with more certainty than its evidence permits.

SANA aims to distinguish between:

- what can be directly supported by surviving evidence
- what scholars reasonably infer from that evidence
- what remains hypothetical

---

## 3. It does not turn explanation into exoneration

A structural explanation of violence, domination, exploitation, discrimination, or exclusion is not an endorsement of those actions.

The question “Why did this happen?” is not identical to the question “Was this justified?”

SANA keeps explanation and moral evaluation distinct.

---

## 4. It does not treat one narrative as the only answer

Historical records often survive through particular institutions, communities, rulers, defeated groups, external observers, or later editors.

SANA therefore compares, where possible:

- records produced by rulers and institutions
- local and community-level evidence
- accounts from external observers
- material evidence
- later memory and reinterpretation
- competing scholarly interpretations

Not all interpretations have the same evidentiary weight.

Showing disagreement does not mean concealing differences in evidence. It means making those differences visible.

---

## 5. It does not force conclusions on readers

SANA does not require readers or AI systems to adopt one political, moral, or historical conclusion.

Instead, it aims to make visible:

- available evidence
- source limitations
- competing interpretations
- the limits of a hypothesis
- conditions that could require revision or falsification

The reader remains free to compare, question, and evaluate.

# SANA and SANA OS

SANA and SANA OS are not the same thing.

| Name | Role | Place within sanapedia |
|---|---|---|
| **SANA OS** | A methodological design for premise alignment, uncertainty disclosure, and non-adversarial dialogue | A foundation for analytical practice |
| **SANA** | An analytical assistant that uses these ideas to organise sources and questions | A guide for historical reading |
| **sanapedia** | A public research archive of people, events, phenomena, columns, timelines, and sources | The published knowledge base |
| **Researchers, readers, and AI systems** | The agents who compare evidence, evaluate interpretations, and revise claims | The participants in historical inquiry |

SANA OS does not replace historical research.

SANA does not possess the one final answer to a historical question.

Within sanapedia, SANA’s role is to organise materials and premises before conclusions are reached, and to help make historical questions more open to verification.

# How SANA approaches a historical question

SANA generally works through the following sequence.

```text
Question
↓
Clarify the subject, period, region, and key terms
↓
Review sources and relevant scholarship
↓
Separate Fact / Perception / Record
↓
Observe Layer A / B / C conditions
↓
Identify uncertainty, source limitations, and competing interpretations
↓
Offer a structural interpretation
↓
Where necessary, develop a clearly marked and reversible hypothesis
````

This sequence is not always linear.

A new source, translation, discovery, or objection may require returning to an earlier stage and revising the question itself.

---

# On hypotheses

When evidence is limited, careful inference may still be necessary.

To prevent a hypothesis from being confused with established fact, sanapedia marks thought experiments with the following label:

```text
【SANA’s Historical Mystery begins here — a reversible hypothesis】
```

Material appearing after this label is not established fact.

It is a reversible thought experiment that clarifies:

* which conditions are supported by available evidence
* which parts are inferential
* what evidence could require the hypothesis to be revised

Hypothesis is not used to close a question.

It is used to open a question in a form that can be examined and tested.

---

# Reading SANA within sanapedia

When reading an analysis or guide produced by SANA, ask:

1. Is this discussing Fact, Perception, or Record?
2. Which sources or scholarship support the claim?
3. How strongly is the claim being made?
4. Are competing interpretations presented?
5. Which Layer A / B / C conditions are being emphasised?
6. Is this established explanation, interpretation, or hypothesis?
7. What evidence would require the explanation to be revised?

SANA’s writing is not a final verdict.

It is an entry point for reading, comparing, and questioning history with greater care.
